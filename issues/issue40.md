## THEME ANALYSIS — ISSUE 40

The theme of the Ekurhuleni Last-Metre Economy is not a story about parcels. It is a story about addresses — about what a delivery business does when the one thing it assumes, a place that can be found, has not been written down, and about the local firms that have built a service out of knowing where people live.

**The deep story:** South Africa has had national address standards since 2009 but no authoritative address register, and the standards are not enforced. In Ekurhuleni's townships the published record shows three responses to that gap: delivery firms founded in the townships themselves, retailer partnerships that hire those firms to reach customers, and parcel-collection points inside local shops.

**Three supporting micro-stories:** Swypa, a delivery marketplace that began in Tembisa in 2019 with four bikes; KasiD, a Tembisa-founded firm that helped launch Spar's SPAR2U home delivery in the area; and Delivery Ka Speed, founded in Hammanskraal, which reported Tsakane, Katlehong and Vosloorus among its East Rand service areas.

**The overlooked place:** the roughly five kilometres between southern Tembisa and Birchleigh North, the northernmost suburb of Kempton Park — the stretch across which one firm's reported flat delivery fee rises from R35 to R50.

**The interesting statistic:** a national courier surcharge of R100 to R150 for township deliveries, reported in 2024, set against a R35 flat fee reported for deliveries in Tembisa in 2022 — the only published township delivery prices this publication could find.

### 1. Editorial

**A Place That Can Be Found**

Researchers writing in January 2026 gave an example of how an address is often supplied in settlements that grew without formal planning: turn right after the first big tree, and the house with the yellow door is the one. The same article reported that South Africa still has no authoritative register of addresses and no clear owner of address data. National address standards have existed since 2009. They are not enforced. An address is nonetheless required to open a bank account or to register as a voter.

The standard recognises three kinds of address. A street address has a street name and a number. A site address has no street name, and an October 2020 article in The Conversation described these as common in townships. An informal address is a verbal description. Municipalities assign addresses and many keep their own records, but there is no national dataset against which an address can be checked.

A delivery business meets that gap at the door. Pargo's chief executive told TechCentral in July 2024 that poor addressing and weak mapping in townships lead to more repeat delivery attempts, and that couriers add a surcharge of around R100 to R150 for security. Order Kasi's operations director said customers who know their area is considered risky often ask drivers to meet them at a police station or petrol station.

This issue looks at what has formed around that gap in Ekurhuleni. It uses published sources only. Where it infers rather than reports, it says so. Several firm-level figures date from 2022 to 2024 and have not been re-verified, and no source found gives a count of delivery riders or collection points for Ekurhuleni as a whole.

---

### 2. The Long Essay

**Finding the Door**

**Section I — The Observation**

What can be observed from the published record is a pattern in service areas and prices. Swypa reported a flat delivery fee of R35 in Tembisa and Soweto in 2022, rising to as much as R50 in Chloorkop, Norkem Park and Birchleigh. KasiD's reported service areas that year included Tembisa, Kempton Park, Kaalfontein and Chloorkop, along with Midrand and Ivory Park. Delivery Ka Speed reported Tsakane, Katlehong and Vosloorus. Pargo reports a network of more than 4,000 collection points nationally, including township spaza shops. None of these sources says how many riders or collection points operate in Ekurhuleni.

**Section II — The Hidden System**

The first layer is the address itself. Where a street name or number does not exist, a description has to do the work. Delivery Ka Speed, which began in Hammanskraal, told TechCentral in 2024 that its local drivers deal with poor addressing by letting customers use landmarks, and that its app makes a landmark description a required field at ordering.

The second layer is the rider who knows the place. Swypa reported growing from four bikes in 2019 to twenty by 2023, when it was adding electric bikes. A trade report said its ordering was first handled manually through WhatsApp for Business, which slowed responses, and later moved to an app. A local newspaper reported that its drivers and centre agents are trained and licensed. KasiD, founded by a Tembisa native, helped launch Spar's SPAR2U home delivery in Tembisa, using three-wheelers, according to a 2023 trade report; a retail-trade report said the partnership enabled roll-out in Kempton Park, Kaalfontein, Soweto, Mamelodi and Glen Marais.

The third layer avoids the door altogether. Pargo says its collection points include township spaza shops, that a shop receives a small fee for each parcel, and that the recipient collects with an ID and a code within eight days. Pargo has said pick-up points can cut delivery costs to rural and outlying areas by as much as 80 percent, and has estimated that 45 percent of its customers are in township and rural areas. These are company figures. A Bob Group executive, quoted in 2024, said pick-up points can overcome the lack of formalised addresses.

> *Where the address register ends, local knowledge becomes the infrastructure.*

**Section III — The Broader Insight**

What follows is this publication's reading, not a finding of any source. In this market the scarce input appears to be knowledge of place, not vehicles. National players have chosen to partner rather than build: Spar worked with KasiD, and Takealot's Township Economy Initiative, launched in 2024 according to Business Report, aims to use last-mile drivers who live in the townships, naming Tembisa, Katlehong, Etwatwa, Vosloorus, Tsakane, Daveyton and Kwa-Thema among its targets.

The gap matters beyond parcels. The 2020 article listed postal and utility services, billing, disaster relief and emergency response among the uses of addresses. Census 2022 counted about 158,000 households in informal dwellings in Ekurhuleni, as reported by Daily News; informal dwellings and unaddressed homes are not the same thing, and no source found counts addresses in Ekurhuleni.

**Section IV — The Antifragile Angle**

The evidence supports a narrower claim than antifragility. The named stress is security risk. Tracker data reported in 2024 showed delivery vehicles more likely to be hijacked than personal cars. Couriers responded with surcharges, and Uber Eats stopped deliveries to parts of Soweto in September 2022, according to Business Report. Order Kasi's operations director said it is hard to find drivers willing to work areas seen as high risk.

Against that, local firms hired local drivers, and a national retailer chose one of them. That sequence is consistent with formal retreat creating demand for local partners. It is not shown. The growth figures for Swypa and KasiD are self-reported, one survey reported township online purchasing rising from 28 percent to 70 percent between 2021 and 2022, and no source separates the effect of a stress from the effect of rising demand. None of the sources reviewed reports rider-safety outcomes for these firms.

### 3. The Hidden Economy

**Three Operations Nobody Is Watching**

**1. The Tembisa Riders**
TEMBISA

Swypa, founded in Tembisa in 2019, carries food, groceries and parcels for formal and informal businesses on motorbikes and scooters. It reported a flat R35 fee in Tembisa and up to R50 in Chloorkop, Norkem Park and Birchleigh in 2022, and grew from four bikes to twenty by 2023. Ordering began on WhatsApp for Business before moving to an app. Its founder said the aim was to let a seller in one corner of Tembisa reach all 52 sections. The sources do not report its margins.

**2. The Retailer's Local Partner**
KAALFONTEIN / KEMPTON PARK

KasiD began as a food-delivery service and helped launch Spar's SPAR2U home delivery in Tembisa, according to a 2023 trade report, which said three-wheelers would be used. A retail-trade report said the arrangement enabled roll-out in Kempton Park and Kaalfontein, among other areas. Spar described it as challenging the idea that townships cannot be adequately served. A Mail & Guardian report, undated in the copy reviewed, put KasiD at 60 drivers, up from 10 at its start.

**3. The Landmark Order**
TSAKANE / KATLEHONG

Delivery Ka Speed began in Hammanskraal as a WhatsApp-based service and now runs an app. A 2022 report listed Tsakane, Katlehong and Vosloorus among its East Rand areas. Its founder told TechCentral in 2024 that local drivers handle poor addressing by accepting landmarks, and that the app requires one at ordering. It reported fulfilment work for Bidvest Waltons, Big Save and Spar's Spar2U app. The sources give no East Rand volumes.

### 4. The Map of the Week

**The Tembisa–Birchleigh Seam**

*26°3′S  28°14′E*

About five kilometres. That is the distance between the published reference point for Tembisa and Birchleigh North, the northernmost suburb of Kempton Park, computed from the coordinates Wikipedia lists for each. Across it, the published record shows: a flat delivery fee of R35 in Tembisa that one firm reported rising to as much as R50 in Chloorkop, Norkem Park and Birchleigh; KasiD service areas that included Tembisa, Kempton Park, Kaalfontein and Chloorkop; and a SPAR2U roll-out reported for Kempton Park and Kaalfontein.

What no source describes is how addresses on either side of the seam are recorded for delivery. The coordinates above are Birchleigh North's; the seam lies to its north. The measurement is the only geography in this section that is calculated rather than reported, and the map is, in effect, a price map.

### 5. The East Rand Number

**R100–R150**

**Surcharge reported for township deliveries by national couriers in 2024 — set against a R35 flat fee reported for deliveries in Tembisa in 2022**

Pargo's chief executive told TechCentral in July 2024 that couriers add around R100 to R150 on top of the delivery fee for township deliveries, to cover security. Swypa reported flat fees of R35 in Tembisa and up to R50 in nearby suburbs in 2022. The comparison is imperfect: the years, services and parcel types differ, and the surcharge is a national figure, not an Ekurhuleni one. It is the only pair of published township delivery prices found. It is a prompt for measurement, not a finding.

---

### 6. Closing Reflection

**Where the Register Ends**

A register of addresses would not remove the risk couriers price in, and it would not by itself create riders or collection points. It would change one thing: whether knowledge of where people live sits in a public record or in a firm's drivers. The published evidence points to the second. How much of that knowledge exists, in which Ekurhuleni sections, and who else may use it, is not counted anywhere this publication could find.
